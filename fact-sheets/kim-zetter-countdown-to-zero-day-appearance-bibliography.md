# Kim Zetter / *Countdown to Zero Day*: Appearance Bibliography 2014–2026

This is a dated, checked list of every findable place Kim Zetter has talked about *Countdown to Zero Day: Stuxnet and the Launch of the World's First Digital Weapon* (Crown, published Nov. 11, 2014), or published new reporting that extends it. It covers print, radio, podcasts, YouTube, and congressional testimony. It was compiled for the Cyber Guild / Cybersecurity Canon community book club with Zetter on November 10, 2026. *Countdown to Zero Day* is a Cybersecurity Canon Hall of Fame book.

**Coverage as of 2026-11-09:** 43 appearances. 32 are direct, including 4 pieces of her own later reporting that extend the book; 6 are adjacent; 5 have a topic or date that couldn't be confirmed. Treat this as a floor, not a census: unrecorded talks, paywalled print, and podcasts with poor show notes are almost certainly missing.

**How it was built:** web search across print, podcast, and event sources, plus a direct harvest of YouTube search results from eight queries. Every candidate video's watch page was then checked for its channel, exact upload date, and whether Zetter and the book or Stuxnet are named in the description. Where an upload date differs from the event date, both are given.

**Highest-value items for future use:**
- **Black Hat USA 2022 keynote**, "Pre-Stuxnet, Post-Stuxnet: Everything Has Changed, Nothing Has Changed." Her own retrospective framing of the book.
- **House testimony, July 22, 2025**, "Stuxnet 15 Years Later." Her most authoritative recent statement ([written statement, docs.house.gov](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/HM/HM08/20250722/118444/HHRG-119-HM08-Wstate-ZetterK-20250722.pdf)).
- **Yahoo News, Sept. 2019 (with Huib Modderkolk).** A Dutch-recruited mole gave Stuxnet its way into Natanz, closing a gap the book left open.
- **Zero Day, May 16, 2026, on Fast16.** Argues Fast16 predates Stuxnet, which challenges the "world's first digital weapon" claim.
- **Stanford CISAC, Feb. 2015.** Said Stuxnet had "very little effect" on Iran's program and that the U.S. lost moral standing on digital weapons.

**Attribution hygiene:** *Stuxnet* is the malware name. *Operation Olympic Games* is the reported operation name. *United States and Israel* is the widely reported sponsorship, never officially acknowledged. *Equation Group* is a vendor-assigned name (Kaspersky, 2015). *Fast16* is separate malware.

**Excluded after checking:** the KwikTips profile video (2025; a third-party channel, not Zetter speaking), the Google Play audiobook preview (narrated by Joe Ochman), and the Kiln and DarkestTech Stuxnet videos (they cite the book; she does not appear).

**Pre-publication (not counted):** SXSW 2014 ([schedule](https://schedule.sxsw.com/2014/events/event_IAP21482)); Wilson Center, June 4, 2014 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QXTRZIoKwU4)); URI Honors Colloquium, Sept. 23, 2014 ([event](https://web.uri.edu/hc/2014-event/kim-zetter/)). Precursor print: WIRED, July 11, 2011, "How Digital Detectives Deciphered Stuxnet" ([Nextgov reprint](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2011/07/how-digital-detectives-deciphered-stuxnet-the-most-menacing-malware-in-history/49400)). The book's copyright page says portions originated there.

**Status:** current as of November 9, 2026. Items marked (U) could not be confirmed and should be checked before citing.
